Introducing R Alongside SAS: Managing Change and Validation in Pharma

18 November 2026

Pharma R&D spent two decades building its own software. Now that more organisations want to move their statistical computing onto open source, the hardest part is rarely the code.

In the first episode of The Coadun Podcast, Esther Gillespie (Jumping Rivers) and James McDermott (Achieve Intelligence) discuss how to move a regulated organisation off SAS without the project overrunning.

Watch the episode below, or skim the key points.

Why pharma ended up building its own software

For two decades, sponsors built their own statistical computing environments on SAS, siloed by function, system and data. Open source and a federated set of connected tools are the way out, and new graduates already arrive knowing R and Python.

The hardest part of leaving SAS isn't the code

Change control governs your software; change management brings people with you. Map every stakeholder, find your champions, and run workshops that show teams their domain knowledge still matters. Our delivery process is built around exactly this.

Why a fixed switch-off date backfires

Moving hundreds of users over by a licence renewal date causes disruption at every step and usually overruns. A phased plan finishes sooner.

Your biggest champions might push back

Early open-source adopters can resist when an enterprise strategy reins in their sandbox freedom. Plan for it from the start.

How to bring reluctant teams with you

Tackle resistance early, get users teaching each other, and record sceptics' concerns in a risk assessment with mitigations. Leave it late, and you end up forcing everyone across on a fixed date.

Are you over-validating?

21 CFR Part 11, Annex 11, and GAMP 5 have led many organisations to validate far more than they need to. The essential goal is an audit trail from the study report back to the source data. Much of the rest is good engineering practice under another name, and tools like Litmus make R package validation far lighter.

What regulators ask for (it's less than you think)

Agency reviewers are independent and ask for less than the industry assumes, something that comes through clearly in forums like PHUSE, where industry and regulators work side by side. Document your risks and reasoning, and there's more flexibility than you'd expect.

The data standards changing clinical research

ICH M11 and CDISC's USDM make study definitions machine-readable, and FHIR APIs, required for US health IT under the 21st Century Cures Act, let patient data flow straight into clinical systems. Getting your architecture right now prepares you for both.
